Presentation Questions

  1. What specific problem did your team identify?
  2. How does your prompt protect the learning objective and match students' proficiency level?
  3. What did you need to verify and correct manually?
  4. Which two teaching actions changed the simulated result?

Key Takeaway

“Using AI effectively is not about generating a text quickly; it is about making a sound teaching decision after checking the result.”

We are not comparing “which team scored highest”; we are exploring why different skills require different strategies and assessment criteria.

If Internet Access Is Unavailable

  1. Open the HTML file on a laptop and projector. You can also provide each team with a local copy.
  2. Work in four teams, with one device per team.
  3. Transfer result codes to the facilitator's device manually, or display reports one by one.
  4. Use the sample “AI responses” provided in Stage 3 of the laboratories instead of live AI generation.

Safety Principles

  • Do not upload students' real names, photographs or identifiable work to an AI tool without a proper legal and institutional basis.
  • Check language accuracy, cultural appropriateness and accessibility.
  • The teacher, not an algorithm, is responsible for assessment decisions.
  • Simulation results support discussion of instructional decisions; they do not demonstrate AI effectiveness.
